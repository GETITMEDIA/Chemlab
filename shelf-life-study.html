Overview

Shelf life studies determine how long a product remains safe, effective, and of acceptable quality under specified storage conditions. These studies are crucial in industries such as pharmaceuticals, food, cosmetics, and chemicals. Shelf life is defined as the time period during which a product retains its intended properties and performance.

Objectives of Shelf Life Studies

  • To establish the expiration date of a product
  • To evaluate stability under environmental conditions
  • To ensure safety and efficacy over time
  • To determine appropriate storage conditions
  • To comply with regulatory requirements

Types of Shelf Life Studies

Type of Study Conditions Duration Purpose / Features
Real-Time Stability Studies Conducted under normal storage conditions Duration equals actual shelf life (months/years) Provides the most accurate data
Accelerated Stability Studies Conducted at elevated temperature and humidity Short-term study Speeds up degradation process; helps predict shelf life quickly
Intermediate Studies Conditions between real-time and accelerated Moderate duration Used when accelerated study data is inconclusive

Factors Affecting Shelf Life

  • Temperature (high heat speeds degradation)
  • Humidity (moisture affects stability)
  • Light exposure (UV can degrade compounds)
  • Oxygen (oxidation reactions)
  • pH levels
  • Packaging material
  • Microbial contamination

Conclusion

Shelf life studies are essential for ensuring that products remain safe, effective, and high-quality throughout their intended use period. Proper design and execution of these studies help manufacturers meet regulatory standards and protect consumers.
